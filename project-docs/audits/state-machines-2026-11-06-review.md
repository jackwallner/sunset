## What survived, ranked

1. B1, alerts shown as on while iOS blocks them.
2. B3, widget fetches suppress alert rebuilds on cold launches.
3. B2, a later grant is not picked up until the next fetch, and the banner goes stale.
4. B5, a double tap in onboarding skips pages, including the Sun+ offer.
5. B4, paywall gives no result after Restore.
6. B6, a fix arriving during a cold-start load is dropped.
7. B9, sunset alerts ignore quiet hours above about 60 N.
8. B7, overlapping reschedules can leave a stale alert.

## Product calls

- 1 is wrong. Free alerts for locked days never reveal a locked score. A sunset alert fires on its own day (`NotificationService.swift:83`) and a sunrise alert at 20:00 the evening before (`:84`, `:129-135`), when that day is already within the two free upcoming days (`OutlookView.swift:20-21`, `SunsetForecast.swift:203-205`). Pending alerts for later days are rebuilt before they fire if the app or BG refresh runs.
- 2 to 8 hold as product calls. 6 (the forced `onFix` installed by "Update location", `LocationService.swift:53`, `SettingsView.swift:87`) also makes every later foreground fix bypass the in-flight guard; still data and battery, not state.

## Missed by the audit (in scope)

- B5's third route: a double tap on "Not now" lands on "Get Started" in the same slot and skips the offer (proved by `testC`).
- B5's watch-page route raises the notification prompt rather than skipping it (proved by `testB`), so the audit's "no notification prompt" and its I1.2 transition row are wrong in detail.
- No other serious state bug found in the audited machines.

## Method changes

- Read the action a repeated tap dispatches, not only the state it reads. B5's write-up says the second tap "sees the already advanced step" and advances again, but `primaryAction` switches on `step` (`OnboardingView.swift:323-340`), so the second tap runs the next page's action. That changes what the user sees (the prompt is shown, and "Not now" becomes "Get Started"). A UI test with `doubleTap()` on the real view settles it in one run; the audit marked it code path without trying.
- Judge a "fix" against the other promises before calling the current behaviour a bug. B8's proposed fix would break the quiet-hour promise for travellers; checking what the user experiences in the new zone, not the planned instant, would have rejected it.
- Trace product-call consequences to fire time. Product call 1 assumed an alert for day 5 is read on day 1; the fire dates in `plan` put every alert inside the free window.
- Estimate frequency from how often the triggering component runs. B3's impact was capped at "repaired within hours" without noticing the widget refetches on its own 3 h cadence and can suppress every cold-launch rebuild.
- Keep test labels in step with the report. The repro's section comments call the quiet-hour tests B1, the trigger replica B2 and the location race B3, while the report numbers them B9, B8 and B6.
